Module 1

Introduction to Communication Systems

Every phone call, Wi‑Fi connection, text message, video stream and satellite transmission depends on a communication system.

Learning Outcomes

  • Describe the purpose of communication systems.
  • Explain how information is transmitted from source to destination.
  • Identify the major components of a communication system.
  • Compare analog and digital systems.
  • Recognize communication systems used every day.

Why Study Communication Systems?

Communication engineering focuses on transferring information accurately, reliably and efficiently.

Examples include:

  • Mobile phones
  • Wi‑Fi networks
  • Radio broadcasting
  • Satellite systems
  • Internet communications
  • Video streaming

Historical Evolution

1830s – Telegraph
1876 – Telephone
1900s – Radio
1950s – Television
1980s – Digital Networks
Today – Wireless & Mobile Systems

Communication System Model

Information Source → Transmitter → Channel → Receiver → Destination

Information Source

Produces information such as speech, images, text or sensor measurements.

Transmitter

Converts information into a signal suitable for transmission.

Channel

Carries the signal through air, cable, optical fibre or satellite links.

Receiver

Recovers the transmitted information.

Destination

The final user or application.

Analog vs Digital Communications

Analog

  • AM radio
  • FM radio
  • Continuous signals

Digital

  • Wi‑Fi
  • 5G cellular systems
  • Internet communications
  • Error correction and encryption

Key Takeaways

  • Communication systems transfer information.
  • Signals represent information.
  • Every communication system has common functional blocks.
  • Modern systems are primarily digital.
