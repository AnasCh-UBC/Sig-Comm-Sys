Introduction to Communication Systems

Everything from mobile phones and Wi‑Fi to satellite communications relies on the same fundamental ideas explored in this course.

Learning Outcomes

  • Describe the purpose of communication systems.
  • Explain how information is transmitted.
  • Identify major communication-system components.
  • Compare analog and digital communication.
  • Recognize communication technologies in everyday life.

Why Study Communication Systems?

Before information can be stored, processed, or displayed, it must first be transmitted. Communication engineers design systems that move information efficiently, accurately, and reliably.

  • Phone calls
  • Text messages
  • Wi‑Fi
  • Bluetooth
  • Radio broadcasting
  • Satellite communication
  • Streaming video

Communication System Model

Information Source → Transmitter → Channel → Receiver → Destination

Information Source

Produces information such as voice, video, text, or sensor data.

Transmitter

Converts information into a suitable signal.

Channel

Carries the signal through air, fiber, copper, or satellite links.

Receiver

Recovers the information from the received signal.

Destination

The final user or application.

Analog vs Digital Communications

Analog

Examples: AM radio, FM radio, traditional broadcasting.

Advantages: Simplicity, continuous signals.

Challenges: Noise sensitivity.

Digital

Examples: Wi‑Fi, 5G, Internet, streaming services.

Advantages: Error correction, compression, encryption, storage.
